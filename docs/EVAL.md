# Evaluation results

Two numbers, and the second is the one that matters.

**Two numbers on the same agent, one curated and one not.**

On 13 real photographs chosen for the demo: **100 percent component recall, 2 trials each, with 0
spurious components in 26 readings.** It never once credited a food that was not there.

On 45 photographs it did not choose, the first five files in each of ten Wikimedia Commons
categories taken in the order the API returned them: **35 of 45, 78 percent.** And the part that
matters more than either figure: **of the 10 it got wrong, 9 credited nothing at all.** When this
agent is wrong on a photograph it has never seen, it stays silent rather than inventing a component,
9 times out of 10. A component invented into a record is a false claim against a federal food
programme; a component left out is a question asked.

**Against the obvious alternative, it recovers meals a claim-time check can only report.** Every
food programme app already validates the claim at month end, and so does the sponsor before paying.
On the same 13 real photographs, 4 of the 6 whole services and 7 of the 7 single foods would not
have been paid as photographed, and for 8 of those 11 the fix is a single item. Tally names it while
the food is on the table. A claim-time check finds the identical shortfalls four weeks later.

**It refuses 13 of 13 adversarial cases, and wrongly refuses 0 of 6 legitimate ones.** A snack one
component short, a state whose ratio table it does not have, an infant meal it does not model, a
food read below its confidence threshold. Refusing everything would score 100 percent on the first
number and make the product worthless, so the six it must credit are the control.

**Proved against the regulation (October 2026).** The one tool added for the *Give your Agent a Tool* challenge, `prove_answer`, checked on 30 questions a provider asks with the answers USDA gives: **26 of 30 wrong answers contradicted, 0 of 30 correct answers wrongly blocked, 0 of 4 off-topic answers wrongly proved.** The same agent three ways, every final answer judged by the solver: wrong answers served 9 of 30 by a general assistant (same model, no Tally, no tool), 3 of 30 by Tally without the tool, 6 of 30 with a two-agent debate, 1 of 30 with the tool. Pointed at Tally's own rulebook it found 1 stale rule in 23; after the fix, 0. On ten held-out questions written after the last change and run once: 8 of 10 wrong answers contradicted, 0 of 10 correct answers blocked. The agent called the tool on 30 of 30 rule questions and 0 of 10 other inputs. Nine wrong answers that try to get proved anyway (ignore the rules, claim authority, restate the limit in the answer or the facts, write the verdict into the answer, JSON, filler, a sponsor override, a question that asks for a yes): 0 of 9 proved, the control proved (`python -m evals.adversarial_eval`). Answers carrying a number in neither the question nor the regulation: general assistant 4 of 30, no tool 2, debate 3, with the tool 2, which is the gap the numbers-from-nowhere check on the page now names. `python -m evals.score` recounts all of it without AWS.

Both are regenerated by scripts in this repository. The refusal eval calls no model and runs in CI
on every push; the vision eval calls Bedrock and is run deliberately.

## Against the obvious alternative

Generated 2026-09-09 19:45 UTC by `python -m evals.baseline_eval`.

The incumbent is not paper. Providers already have apps that record meals and check them when the month is filed, and the sponsoring organisation runs the same check again before it pays. Neither check is broken. Neither one helps, because by the time either runs the food has been eaten and the record cannot change.

Tally runs the identical rule at the table. This measures what moving it there is worth, on the thirteen real photographs in `data/plates`, judged as the meal each depicts.

**4 of the 6 photographs that show a whole service would not have been paid as photographed**, and **7 of the 7 single foods**, which are real things to put out for a snack and do not satisfy a pattern on their own.

For **8 of those 11, the fix is one item.** Tally names it while the food is still on the table. A claim-time check finds exactly the same shortfalls four weeks later, when nothing can be done about any of them.

At this year's published rates, for a home with 6 children at the lower tier, those 11 services are **101.76 dollars** that a claim-time check reports and a table-time check recovers.

The two rows are kept apart on purpose. Counting a single banana beside a full lunch tray as though they were the same kind of evidence would inflate the number.
